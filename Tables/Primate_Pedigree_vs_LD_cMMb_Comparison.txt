PRIMATE PEDIGREE-VS-LD cM/Mb COMPARISON
Does pedigree-based cM/Mb read systematically higher than LD-based cM/Mb?
Companion note to Primate_cMMb_Baseline_Average.txt and Primate_NCO_Baseline_Average.txt
Computed from Central_Comparison_Table.xlsx

PURPOSE

Noticed while writing up Cercopithecidae' within-species disagreement
(rhesus macaque, whose 7 cM/Mb estimates span 0.107-0.88): the single
highest estimate is the pedigree-based one, and every LD-based estimate for
the same species reads lower, regardless of population (Indian captive vs.
wild Chinese-origin) or estimator (LDhat vs. pyrho). This raised an obvious
follow-up question -- is "pedigree reads higher than LD" a genuine, general
pattern across this review, or just something specific to one internally
messy macaque dataset? This note checks every other species in the review
with both a pedigree-based and an LD-based cM/Mb estimate to find out.

METHOD

Identified every species in this review with both a pedigree-based AND an
LD-based cM/Mb estimate (5 species: rhesus macaque, vervet, titi monkey,
aye-aye, olive baboon). For each, checked whether the LD-based estimate was
calibrated independently (its own Ne, its own map-length target) or
deliberately rescaled by the original study's authors to match their own
companion pedigree study's total map length -- the same "circular vs.
defensible calibration" distinction established in
Primate_cMMb_Baseline_Average.txt's CIRCULARITY CHECK section (Wall et al.
2022 baboon, Versoza et al. 2025 aye-aye, and Soni et al. 2026 titi monkey
all explicitly rescale their own LD map to match their own pedigree map
length; macaque and vervet's LD estimates do not).

This distinction matters a great deal here specifically: a rescaled
comparison is, by construction, biased toward agreement, so it can at most
show whether a residual gap *survives* that bias -- it cannot on its own
demonstrate a real difference the way an independent comparison can.

RESULT -- ALL 5 SPECIES, RANKED BY INDEPENDENCE

  Rhesus macaque (Macaca mulatta) -- INDEPENDENT
    Pedigree (WGS, Indian captive, Versoza et al. 2024):            0.88
    LD (pyrho, same population, Versoza et al. 2024 companion map): 0.107  (8.22x lower)
    LD (LDhat, Indian-origin, Xue et al. 2020):                     0.448  (1.96x lower)
    LD (pyrho, wild Chinese-origin, Terbot et al. 2025):            0.694  (1.27x lower)

  Vervet (Chlorocebus sabaeus) -- INDEPENDENT
    Pedigree (microsatellite, Jasinska et al. 2007):  1.141
    LD (LDhat, Pfeifer 2020, independently-validated Ne): 0.452  (2.52x lower)

  Titi monkey (Plecturocebus cupreus) -- RESCALED (LD forced toward pedigree total)
    Pedigree (WGS, Versoza et al. 2026):                          1.02
    LD (LDhelmet, rescaled to pedigree map length, Soni et al. 2026): 0.937  (1.09x lower)
    LD (LDhat, rescaled to pedigree map length, Soni et al. 2026):     0.824  (1.24x lower)

  Aye-aye (Daubentonia madagascariensis) -- RESCALED
    Pedigree (WGS, Versoza et al. 2025):                        0.85
    LD (pyrho, rescaled to pedigree map length, Soni et al. 2025): 0.669  (1.27x lower)

  Olive baboon (Papio anubis) -- RESCALED
    Pedigree (WGS, Wall et al. 2022):                                  0.8851
    LD (pyrho, rescaled to match pedigree to within 0.02%, Wall et al. 2022): 0.8851  (1.00x, matches)

FINDING 1 -- BOTH INDEPENDENT COMPARISONS AGREE: PEDIGREE READS HIGHER

Macaque and vervet are the only two species in this review where an LD-based
cM/Mb estimate was calibrated on its own terms rather than forced toward the
pedigree figure -- and both show pedigree substantially above every LD-based
estimate available (1.27-8.22x for macaque depending on which of 3 LD
estimates is used; 2.52x for vervet). This is a small sample (n=2 independent
species), but it is not a single-dataset artifact: two different species,
two different pedigree method types (WGS vs. classical microsatellite), and
four different LD-based estimates (2 estimators, 2 populations for macaque
alone) all point the same direction.

FINDING 2 -- THE DIRECTION NEVER REVERSES, EVEN UNDER DELIBERATE RESCALING

The 3 rescaled comparisons (titi monkey, aye-aye, baboon) are compromised
for testing this question on their own -- their LD maps were deliberately
forced toward the pedigree total -- but they are still informative as a
weaker check: if rescaling could easily flip pedigree below LD, or if the
true relationship were noisy/directionless, some of these 3 would be
expected to land on the other side by chance. None do. Titi monkey and
aye-aye both retain a residual pedigree-higher gap (9-27%) even after their
LD map was explicitly recalibrated toward the pedigree figure; only baboon's
rescaling closed the gap essentially completely (0.02% difference, the
single tightest cM/Mb agreement anywhere in this review). All 5 of 5 species
point the same direction; 0 of 5 reverse it.

FINDING 3 -- BABOON'S NEAR-PERFECT MATCH IS ITSELF INFORMATIVE

Baboon's 0.02% agreement demonstrates that an LD-based map CAN be calibrated
to land essentially exactly on a pedigree figure -- which argues against
"LD-based methods are structurally incapable of reaching pedigree-level
values" as a full explanation for the gap seen elsewhere. If that were the
whole story, baboon's rescaling should have left some structural residual
too. It didn't. This makes the persistent gap in titi monkey and aye-aye
(despite the same rescaling strategy Wall et al. used successfully for
baboon) a genuine puzzle rather than an expected consequence of the method
-- worth flagging in the text as its own small mystery, not folded into the
macaque/vervet finding as though it's the same phenomenon with the same
explanation.

TWO CANDIDATE EXPLANATIONS (NOT MUTUALLY EXCLUSIVE, NOT ADJUDICATED HERE)

1. Methodological: LD-based (population-scaled, coalescent) estimates are
   known in the broader recombination-genetics literature to be biased
   downward in genomic regions affected by linked or background selection --
   selection distorts haplotype/LD patterns in ways that mimic locally
   reduced recombination, and that bias would be expected to apply generally
   across species, not just to the ones flagged here.
2. Genuinely historical vs. contemporary rates: pedigree studies measure the
   most recent generation(s) directly; LD-based methods integrate
   recombination signal over many thousands of generations of coalescent
   history. If recombination rate has trended upward recently in these
   lineages, pedigree-based figures would read higher than LD-based ones as
   a real biological difference, not an artifact.

This review's own standing methodological caveat -- that cM/Mb from
LD-based, pedigree-based, and cytogenetic methods are not strictly the same
quantity -- already anticipates that these numbers needn't agree; what this
note adds is the first systematic empirical look, across every paired case
this review has, at the direction and rough magnitude of that disagreement,
rather than leaving it as a qualitative caveat alone.

CAVEATS

- Small sample: 5 species total, only 2 with a genuinely independent
  comparison. A finding this consistent (5 of 5 same direction) is
  suggestive, not proof, at n=2 independent cases -- treat as a hypothesis
  worth stating plainly in the text, not a settled result.
- "Pedigree" itself isn't one method here: WGS-based pedigree calling
  (macaque, titi monkey, aye-aye, baboon) and classical microsatellite
  pedigree mapping (vervet) are different technologies with different
  resolution -- the pattern holds across both, which is reassuring, but it
  means the comparison set isn't perfectly homogeneous on the pedigree side
  either.
- "LD-based" also spans 3 different estimators (LDhat, LDhelmet, pyrho) with
  their own known behavioral differences (e.g. pyrho's block-penalty
  smoothing, which also produces mechanically different Gini values -- see
  Central_Comparison_Table.xlsx's Notes sheet) -- some of the within-species
  LD spread (e.g. macaque's 0.107 vs. 0.694) is very likely
  estimator/population choice, not just "LD vs. pedigree."
- Directionality only, not effect size: the 3 rescaled comparisons cannot be
  used to estimate *how much* pedigree exceeds LD in general, only whether
  the sign survives deliberate pressure toward agreement -- their magnitudes
  (9-27% gaps) should not be pooled with the independent comparisons' much
  larger gaps (127-822%) into a single "typical size of the effect" figure.

BOTTOM LINE

Every species in this review with both a pedigree-based and an LD-based
cM/Mb estimate reads pedigree at or above LD -- never the reverse -- across
5 species, 2 of them fully independent comparisons (macaque, vervet) and 3
deliberately biased toward agreement by the original authors' own rescaling
(titi monkey, aye-aye, baboon). The pattern survives that bias in 2 of the 3
rescaled cases. This is too small a sample to adjudicate between a
methodological explanation (linked-selection bias in LD-based estimates)
and a genuine historical-vs-contemporary rate difference, but the
consistency of direction across every available case makes this worth
reporting as a real, if preliminary, finding of this review -- not just
restating the already-standard "these methods measure different things"
caveat.

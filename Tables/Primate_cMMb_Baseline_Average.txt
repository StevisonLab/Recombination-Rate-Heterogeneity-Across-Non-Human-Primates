PRIMATE cM/Mb BASELINE AVERAGE
Context for the commonly-used "~1 cM/Mb" default assumption
Computed from Central_Comparison_Table.xlsx

PURPOSE

Many papers (including, in the companion Gibbon-Recombination-Rate repository,
Okhovat et al. 2020's choice of "a standard rate of 1x10-8 per site per
generation" = 1 cM/Mb) use a flat ~1 cM/Mb default for primate species that
lack their own recombination map. This note checks that default against the
actual spread of empirically estimated cM/Mb values assembled across this
review, and reports how much variation to expect around it. It is a
standalone analytical note for the discussion/text of the chapter, not a new
synthesis figure -- the scope, methods, markers, and physical scales behind
these numbers are too heterogeneous to justify a cross-species figure beyond
what Figure 1 already does with the method column retained.

METHOD

One value per species (not per study): where a species has multiple
published/recomputed cM/Mb estimates, they are averaged into a single
per-species figure first, so that species with more studies (baboon, titi
monkey, vervet, pygmy marmoset, cotton-top tamarin, aye-aye) don't get extra
weight in the cross-species average relative to single-estimate species.

Macaca mulatta is the one species in this review measured across two
genuinely distinct populations with different demographic histories
(Indian-origin captive colonies vs. wild Chinese-origin animals), rather than
one population measured by multiple methods -- so it is represented as TWO
entries below (Indian-origin / Chinese-origin), the same treatment used for
Nigerian vs. western chimp (two populations of one species, each its own
row), not folded into one averaged figure and not excluded (see Finding 3).

Species/populations included: 23. Seven species with a known-unreliable
cM/Mb value are excluded entirely from this average (see "Excluded species"
below), even though they remain fully documented in
Central_Comparison_Table.xlsx and are still shown in Figure 2A's
crossover-assurance panel and Figure 1's tree (via the crossover-assurance
ring), both of which are unaffected by the issue that excludes them here.

CIRCULARITY CHECK -- WHY MOST "CALIBRATED" VALUES ARE STILL USABLE HERE

Several LD-based studies calibrate their own cM/Mb by forcing the total
genetic map length to match an assumed-equal-to-human map (the McVean et al.
2004 approach) -- a legitimate concern for a "does this match the ~1 cM/Mb
human-based default" comparison, since including a value that was calibrated
TO roughly match human recombination rate would bias the answer by
construction.

Stevison et al. (2016)'s three great apes (bonobo, Nigerian chimp, western
gorilla) were originally like this -- their own published figure is a flat
~1.193 cM/Mb for all three species, forced by construction. This review
replaces that circular figure with a genuinely independent recalculation
using a species-specific point estimate of Ne from a dedicated demographic
study, rather than the Prado-Martinez et al. (2013) range midpoint (the same
upgrade pattern used for gibbon, where Veeramah et al. 2015's independent
point-estimate Ne replaced Carbone et al. 2014's ancestral, non-species-
specific figure): bonobo Ne=23,230 (Han et al. 2024 Curr Biol, common-
ancestor estimate), Nigerian chimp Ne=27,795 (de Manuel et al. 2016 Science,
per-subspecies estimate, sourced via Fontsere et al. 2022 Cell Genomics's own
citation of de Manuel et al. 2016), western gorilla Ne=31,777 (McManus et al.
2015 MBE, ancestral/long-duration epoch of the best-fit 3-epoch demographic
model). Resulting cM/Mb: bonobo 0.6893, Nigerian chimp 0.7199, western
gorilla 0.7424.

Auton et al. 2012's western chimp figure originally used the paper's own
Ne=11,413 (cM/Mb=1.5122) -- a similar but looser calibration than Stevison
2016's (Ne fit by regression rather than forced to an exact target). The same
de Manuel et al. 2016 / Fontsere et al. 2022 source used for Nigerian chimp
above also gives a genuine point estimate for western chimp, Ne=17,378,
applied to this review's own back-solved raw rho/kb (0.6903) to give
cM/Mb=0.9931 -- placing western chimp on the same non-circular footing as the
other three great apes (further averaged here with its own chiasma-count
estimate; see caveat below).

CAVEAT ON THE WESTERN CHIMP AVERAGE: Pan troglodytes verus averages two
estimates of very different character -- a modern LD-based population-genomic
estimate (Auton et al. 2012, independent Ne) and a classical chiasma count
whose subspecies attribution is itself a historical-plausibility inference,
not a confirmed citation. This review's own stated methodology (average all
available per-species estimates) is applied consistently here as for every
other multi-estimate species, but flagged explicitly: unlike, say, baboon's
two microsatellite pedigree maps (comparable vintage and method), these two
chimp estimates differ enough in kind that a reader may reasonably want to
weight them differently, or use the LD-based value (0.9931) alone.

FINDING 1 -- THE ~1 cM/Mb DEFAULT READS AS A MODEST OVERESTIMATE, NOT JUST A
BALLPARK

With this 23-species/population sample, the mean (0.84) sits about 16% below
the commonly used 1 cM/Mb default, and the median (0.82, more robust to the
gibbon outlier) runs about 18% below it. The full range (0.63-1.55, a
2.5-fold spread) still shows any single species could plausibly sit well
outside +/-20% of the default -- treat 1 cM/Mb as accurate to within roughly
a factor of 1.5-2x in either direction, not as a precise figure, but with a
clear central tendency that undershoots the round-number default
specifically, not just varies around it.

FINDING 2 -- GIBBON IS THE HIGHEST VALUE, WITH MACAQUE'S CHINESE-ORIGIN
POPULATION A CLOSE SECOND

Nomascus leucogenys (this review's own unpublished LD map, Ne = 57,000 from
Veeramah et al. 2015) is the single highest value in the entire review at
1.5498 cM/Mb -- about 1.97x the per-species median. This is a genuine,
notable finding -- not previously reported in the literature for gibbon
specifically -- worth stating plainly in the text, though see the
Ne-sensitivity caveat in the companion Gibbon-Recombination-Rate repository
(gibbon's rank as "the highest" specifically depends on using Veeramah et
al. (2015)'s median Ne or tighter; it remains top-5 of individual study
estimates under every Ne choice tested, but the single-highest claim is
Ne-choice-dependent).

Macaca mulatta (Chinese-origin)'s bias-corrected 1.21 cM/Mb narrows this
picture: the gap to second place is just 0.34 cM/Mb (1.5498 vs. 1.21), and
the bulk of the remaining distribution (positions 3 through 23) spans
0.63-0.94, a separate, lower cluster that both gibbon and Chinese-origin
macaque sit above. Gibbon is still the single highest value in this review,
but it is not a solitary outlier -- it has real company near the top.

FINDING 3 -- MACAQUE: TWO POPULATIONS, NOT ONE INCONSISTENT SPECIES

Macaca mulatta has 8 separate cM/Mb estimates across this review, spanning
an 8-fold range (0.107-1.21):

  0.107        Indian-origin, captive (LD-based, pyrho) -- Versoza et al.
               2024 GBE (companion map)
  0.448 +/- 0.286  Indian-origin (LD-based, LDhat) -- Xue et al. 2020 PLoS ONE
  0.6474       Indian-origin/unspecified (Cytogenetic, Chiasma) --
               Pardo-Manuel de Villena & Sapienza 2001 Mamm Genome
  0.6645       Indian-origin/unspecified (Cytogenetic, MLH1) -- Hassold et
               al. 2009 Cytogenet Genome Res
  0.6909       unspecified population, sex-averaged (Cytogenetic, Chiasma,
               sex-specific source) -- Burt, Bell & Harvey 1991 J Evol Biol,
               via Mank 2009 Table 1
  0.6936       wild, Chinese-origin (LD-based, pyrho, this review's raw
               recomputation) -- Terbot, Calahorra-Oliart, Versoza et al.
               2025 Am J Primatol (paper's own published figure: 0.79)
  0.698        unspecified population (pedigree microsatellite) -- Rogers
               et al. 2006 Genomics
  0.88         Indian-origin, captive (pedigree WGS) -- Versoza et al. 2024
               GBE

Reframing this by population resolves most of the apparent disagreement:
Terbot et al. (2025)'s re-evaluation of Chinese-origin macaque demographic
history describes a genuinely different population from every other estimate
in this list (all Indian-origin), with its own distinct demographic
trajectory including a severe contraction ~3,000 generations ago -- not a
repeated measurement of the same population by a different method. Once
reframed this way, the "8-fold disagreement" resolves mostly into two
clusters along population lines: 5 Indian-origin estimates (0.107-0.88) and
Chinese-origin's own progression from raw pyrho (0.6936/0.79) to the paper's
own bias-corrected estimate (1.21, correcting for pyrho's known
underestimation under population contraction, per the paper's own
coalescent-simulation validation).

DECISION: Macaca mulatta enters the cross-species average as TWO
population-specific entries, the same treatment used for Nigerian vs.
western chimp (two populations of one species, each its own row, not
averaged together and not excluded):

  Macaca mulatta, Indian-origin:  0.88 cM/Mb (Versoza et al. 2024 pedigree
    WGS -- the most direct method available for this population, preferred
    over this same paper's own companion LD estimate (0.107) per this
    review's precedence rule of direct observation over LD-based estimates
    where both exist)

  Macaca mulatta, Chinese-origin: 1.21 cM/Mb (Terbot et al. 2025's own
    bias-corrected estimate -- the best available figure for this
    population, since no pedigree data exists for Chinese-origin macaques
    in this review)

The other 5 estimates are not deleted from this review -- they remain fully
documented in Central_Comparison_Table.xlsx as the full within-population
method-comparison picture, just no longer the number(s) feeding this
cross-species average.

Note on Xue et al. 2020 specifically: that paper's own raw/unbinned rho/kb
(0.4877) remains ~4.3x higher than its own stated rho (0.112) -- an
unresolved raw-vs-binned discrepancy an Ne swap cannot fix, so the paper's
own published cM/Mb is used for that row rather than the discrepant
recomputed value.

CAVEATS THAT APPLY TO ALL NUMBERS ABOVE

- Method heterogeneity: the 23-species/population average mixes LD-based
  (population-scaled, historical), pedigree-based (direct meiotic count), a
  bias-corrected LD estimate (Macaca mulatta Chinese-origin -- see Finding
  3), and cytogenetic (MLH1 foci or classical chiasma counts) methods.
  Western chimp's own average blends an LD-based and a cytogenetic estimate
  (see caveat above); every other cytogenetic contribution is a
  cytogenetic-only species: pygmy marmoset, Japanese macaque, Celebes
  crested macaque, ring-tailed lemur, and all 3 Eulemur fulvus taxa (5
  species, 6 rows). These are not strictly the same quantity as the
  LD/pedigree entries (see the central table's methodological caveat) --
  averaging across them for a ballpark figure is defensible for a single
  summary number, but should not be read as though all 23 entries were
  measured the same way.
- Macaca mulatta is the one entry in this average representing a
  population, not a species-level average the way every other multi-
  estimate entry does (e.g. titi monkey's "3 studies averaged" is 3 studies
  of the same population) -- its two rows are two different populations of
  the same species with different demographic histories, intentionally kept
  separate rather than combined. If a reader wants a single "macaque" number
  instead of two population-specific ones, averaging the two (0.88 and
  1.21 -> 1.045) would be defensible but was not done here, matching the
  same choice made for Nigerian vs. western chimp.
- Marker density and physical scale differ substantially across studies
  (SNP arrays vs. whole genomes; different bin sizes and genome builds) --
  not standardized here beyond each study's own reported/recomputed
  genome-wide average.
- Several LD-based values remain calibrated rather than fully independent
  even after the corrections above (see CIRCULARITY CHECK) -- flagged
  per-species in Central_Comparison_Table.xlsx. Macaca mulatta Chinese-
  origin's value is a further variant of this concern: it is the paper's
  own bias-corrected estimate, not a directly measured or independently
  calibrated figure -- see Finding 3.
- Cross-species/genus karyotype or physical-genome-length substitutions
  underlie several of the cytogenetic cM/Mb values in this average
  (documented per-row in Central_Comparison_Table.xlsx) -- these affect the
  physical-length denominator, not the crossover count itself.
- This average will shift any time a per-species value in this review is
  revised -- rerun this note's computation against the current
  Central_Comparison_Table.xlsx before quoting the mean/median in a final
  draft.

PRDM9 ZINC-FINGER ARRAY SIZE vs. GINI COEFFICIENT — HYPOTHESIS TEST
Does a smaller PRDM9 zinc-finger array predict a more even (lower Gini)
genome-wide recombination distribution?
Companion note to Figure 5 (PRDM9_ZF_bubbleplot) and Figure 2 (Gini dotplot)

HYPOTHESIS

Xue et al. (2020), reporting an unusually low and unusually even recombination
landscape in rhesus macaque, noted that the macaque PRDM9 zinc-finger (ZF)
domain has fewer amino acids than chimpanzee or human's, and proposed that a
shorter array might let PRDM9 bind more genomic sites, producing a more even
distribution than in species with a longer array. This chapter tests whether
that relationship generalizes across a multi-species dataset rather than
remaining a single-species post hoc explanation.

DATA

Real, species-matched PRDM9 zinc-finger counts assembled from three
independent sources (see Figure 5 / prdm9_zf_alleles.csv for full
per-allele detail and source attribution): Schwartz et al. (2014)'s own
Supplementary Data 1 (parsed at the individual-allele level, not their
pooled summary figure), an unpublished allele set sequenced by Ségurel &
Przeworski, and new manual annotation (tBLASTn/GeneWise/Princeton-ZF-tool)
against each species' own genome assembly for species missing from both
published sources.

RESULT

Across all nine species with both a Gini coefficient and a ZF count, there
is no support for the hypothesis that a smaller ZF array predicts a more
even recombination landscape:

  Pearson r = 0.180  (n=9, p=0.643)

Restricting to the seven species with an LDhat-based Gini (dropping Papio
anubis and Daubentonia madagascariensis, both pyrho-based — see the
estimator caveat below) reverses the sign entirely: r = -0.343 (n=7,
p=0.451). Neither subset reaches significance, and the fact that they don't
even agree on direction reinforces the same conclusion from a second angle:
this dataset shows no detectable relationship between ZF array size and
genome-wide recombination evenness in either direction.

WHY THIS IS CONSISTENT WITH PRDM9 STRUCTURAL BIOLOGY

Each zinc finger domain contributes roughly 3 bp to the DNA-binding motif.
To reach the 13-15 bp motif length typically associated with PRDM9 binding,
an array of only about 4-5 zinc fingers is already sufficient — every
species in this table's array (7-15.6 fingers) is well above that floor, so
array-size differences within this range are not expected to translate
directly into differences in binding specificity or site number. There is
also a structural asymmetry worth noting: the zinc fingers at the two ends
of the array are more conserved across species than the internal fingers
(Schwartz et al. 2014), meaning that as an array shrinks, it is disproportionately
losing the more variable, species-specific internal fingers rather than
uniformly losing binding capacity — a further reason not to expect a simple
linear relationship between raw array length and binding behavior.

CAVEATS

- Small sample (n=9, or n=7 restricted). A null result at this sample size
  cannot rule out a real but modest effect — it can only say that this
  dataset provides no support for the hypothesis as stated.
- Macaque's own Gini is not uniformly low across estimators in this
  review — the LDhat-based value used here (0.7944) sits mid-to-high in
  the overall Gini distribution; it is only the pyrho-based macaque
  estimates elsewhere in this review that read low, and pyrho's own
  block-penalty smoothing is independently known to depress Gini regardless
  of biology (see Central_Comparison_Table.xlsx's Notes sheet). The original
  macaque observation this hypothesis was built to explain may itself be
  partly an estimator artifact rather than a genuine biological outlier.
- Only one species among this review's 10 Gini species (Plecturocebus
  cupreus) is missing from this table — no annotated PRDM9 exists in its
  genome assembly and no allele-level sequencing exists in either source
  used here.
- Gorilla and bonobo ZF counts are averaged across multiple alleles from
  different individuals/sources rather than a single representative value —
  PRDM9 is known to be highly polymorphic within species, so any
  single-value summary loses real within-species variation that a
  correlation like this cannot use.

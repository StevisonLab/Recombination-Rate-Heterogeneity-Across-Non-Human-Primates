PRDM9 ZINC-FINGER ARRAY SIZE vs. GINI COEFFICIENT — HYPOTHESIS TEST
Does a shorter PRDM9 zinc-finger array predict a more even (lower Gini)
genome-wide recombination distribution?
Companion note to Figure 5 (PRDM9_ZF_bubbleplot) and Figure 2 (Gini dotplot)

HYPOTHESIS

Xue et al. (2020), in their cross-species PRDM9 zinc-finger (ZF) domain
comparison, note that the rhesus macaque PRDM9 ZF domain has fewer amino
acids than chimpanzee or human, which "might result in shorter DNA binding
sites in rhesus. If macaque PRDM9 binds to more genomic sites than human
PRDM9, this may produce a more even distribution of recombination rates
across [the] genome compared with humans." This is offered as a candidate
explanation for macaque's own recombination-rate distribution specifically,
not extended to any other species by the original authors. This note tests
whether the relationship generalizes across this review's own multi-species
dataset.

A PRELIMINARY PROBLEM WITH THE PREMISE

Before testing any correlation, it's worth noting that macaque's own Gini
isn't consistently low in this review's data in the first place. The
Xue et al. LDhat-based value (0.7944) sits mid-to-high in this review's
whole Gini distribution — it's only the two pyrho-based macaque estimates
(0.6144, 0.2813) that read low, and this review has already established
elsewhere (see Master_Comparison_Table.xlsx's Notes sheet) that pyrho's
block-penalty smoothing mechanically depresses Gini independent of biology.
The "macaque is unusually even" observation the hypothesis was originally
built to explain is therefore itself likely an estimator artifact,
undermining the premise before the ZF-count mechanism is even tested.

There is also a biological objection worth stating plainly: each C2H2 zinc
finger contacts roughly 3 bp of DNA, so achieving a genome-unique address in
a ~3 Gb genome only requires about 6 fingers (4^18 ~ 6.8x10^10 > 3x10^9) — a
modestly shorter array is unlikely to be anywhere near that threshold, so
there's no obvious reason it should bind MORE sites rather than simply
fewer, still-highly-specific ones. The more defensible version of the
argument is about recognized motif length, not binding affinity — but even
that doesn't show an obvious scaling with array size across this review's
own directly-discovered motifs (marmoset's 15-mer, titi monkey's ~15-mer,
human's 13- and 17-bp submotifs all cluster in a narrow range despite quite
different source-species ZF counts).

DATA

Real, species-matched PRDM9 zinc-finger counts assembled from three
independent sources (see Figure 5 / prdm9_zf_alleles.csv for full
per-allele detail and source attribution): (1) new tBLASTn/GeneWise/
Princeton-ZF-tool annotation work done directly against each species' own
genome assembly; (2) Ségurel & Przeworski's unpublished directly-sequenced
allele set; (3) Schwartz et al. (2014)'s own Supplementary Data 1 (parsed
at the individual-allele level, not their pooled summary figure). Two
data-quality corrections were made during assembly: one Ségurel/Przeworski
macaque allele header was mislabeled (its exact sequence length corresponds
to 9 zinc fingers, not the labeled 8), and macaque's representative value
was set to 8 (the modal count across all 6 real alleles, and the same
figure Xue et al. themselves cite) rather than Schwartz's own single,
discrepant macaque allele (11, n=1).

RESULT

  All 9 species:                    Pearson r = 0.180  (n=9, p=0.643)
  Restricted to 7 LDhat-only species: Pearson r = -0.343 (n=7, p=0.451)

The LDhat-only restriction excludes Papio anubis and Daubentonia
madagascariensis, both pyrho-based Gini — the same estimator confound
flagged above for the macaque Gini values, so they don't belong in an
unqualified regression alongside seven LDhat values.

INTERPRETATION

Neither correlation clears conventional significance, and — tellingly — the
two subsets don't even agree on the SIGN of the relationship (positive for
all 9 species, negative when restricted to LDhat-only). That instability, on
top of the non-significance, is about as clean a null result as this test
can produce: with real, species-matched zinc-finger counts for 9 of this
review's 10 Gini species, there is no detectable relationship between PRDM9
zinc-finger array size and genome-wide recombination evenness in this
review's data, in either direction.

CAVEATS

- Small sample (n=9, or n=7 restricted). A null result at this sample size
  cannot rule out a real but modest effect — it can only say that this
  review's data provides no support for the hypothesis as stated.
- Only one species (Plecturocebus cupreus) among this review's 10 Gini
  species is missing from this table entirely — no annotated PRDM9 exists
  in its genome assembly and no allele-level sequencing exists in either
  Schwartz et al. (2014) or the Ségurel/Przeworski dataset.
- Gorilla and bonobo ZF counts are averaged across multiple alleles from
  different individuals/sources (Schwartz 2014 + Ségurel/Przeworski for
  gorilla; Schwartz 2014 alone, n=4 range 7-8, for bonobo) rather than a
  single representative value — this review's own PRDM9 loci are known to
  be highly polymorphic within species, so any single-value summary loses
  real within-species variation that a correlation like this cannot use.
